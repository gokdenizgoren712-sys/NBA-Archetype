14 Loading, empty, offline 8d
8dLoading · empty · offline — the three states, on web
LOADING Skeletons only. The card skeleton keeps its notches, both crest columns, the score block and five heat bars — so nothing shifts when data lands.
EMPTY Names one action and stops.
No matches match those filters.
Minimum heat is at 4.5 and only six matches have cleared that this week.
Lower it to 3.0
No sample fixtures, no suggested friends, no filler cards.
OFFLINE Cached cards dim to 62%, never disappear.
You're offline
Your rating is saved here and will upload when you reconnect.
WHY SKELETONS, NOT SPINNERS
A spinner says "wait" and tells you nothing. A skeleton that keeps the card's real geometry — notches, two crest columns, score block, five heat bars — means the page never jumps when data lands, and the shape is recognisable before the content exists.
ERROR STATES — STILL OPEN
A rating that fails to save, a 404 match, a stale broadcast listing, a session that expires mid-rating. Offline promises the upload will happen — nothing yet says what a rejected upload looks like. BUILD.md §2.3.
15 Design notes — the reasoning, by turn newest first
16 Web catches up to the match lifecycle

Mobile got all five phases of the sheet; web drew one. Seven screens close it — and two of them were P0, not the P2 I had filed them as: a web user who opens a live match or a finished-but-unrated one was hitting a layout meant for something else.

THE PANEL CARRIES ALL FIVE
Same three tabs, same order, at every phase — only the contents and the footer action change. The Inspector never grows or loses a tab, because a panel whose tab count depends on kick-off time is a panel you cannot learn.
NO XIExpected heat, squads — 16c
PRECountdown, who is coming — 16a
LIVEThe pitch — 15w · the room — 7d
FT, UNRATEDThe invitation — 15x · the record — 16b
FT, RATEDEntry and crowd — 7c
PICKER IS A DIALOG, COMPOSER IS NOT
The players picker (15y) is a centred dialog like quick-rate: you are choosing from a fixed list and then leaving. The composer (15z) stays in the panel, because a review is a field on your entry and the entry lives there — pulling it into an overlay would imply it is a separate object you publish.
Desktop adds one thing to the composer: saves as you type. A phone cannot promise that honestly mid-tunnel; a browser can.
THE PULSE BECOMES A RECORD
After full time (16b) the chart stops being live and starts being how the night went — KO to FT, chat closed, thread kept. Nothing is deleted when a match ends; the room becomes the archive of itself.

14 Getting in, and getting told — web P2 closed

The last two web surfaces. First run mattered more than its P2 label suggested — without it the site assumed a signed-in user, so the one person who can't get in was the one we hadn't designed for. Mobile needs three screens for it (4g, 4h, 2r); web needs one, because a 1440px canvas can hold a promise and a choice at the same time.

THREE STEPS BECOME ONE SCREEN
Mobile sequences connect → follow → promise because each one fills a phone. On 14a the promise is the left half and permanent, the choice is the right half and live — so you read why you're here while you pick, instead of agreeing to something you've forgotten by step three.
The step counter stays. One screen, still three decisions — collapsing them shouldn't hide that Primary Arch is a real account link.
THE SHIELD, ASKED UP FRONT
BUILD.md §3 flagged that first run never mentions the spoiler shield — the product's most distinctive setting, buried in Settings. 14a asks it as the third choice, because a user who finds it later has already been spoiled once.
NOTIFICATIONS ARE A DROPDOWN
Not the Inspector — that panel is for things you're studying. A notification is something you glance at and dismiss, so 14b hangs off the header bell at 420px and keeps the same four channel groups and three spoiler degrees as 13a.
12 The last three — web P1 closed

Entity drawer, lists, and The Hunt. After these, every surface a user can reach on web has a design — what's left in BUILD.md is P2 and written decisions, not screens. The one real call here: a club is not a page, it's the Inspector, because you look a club up while you're in the middle of something else.

A CLUB IS A LOOKUP, NOT A DESTINATION
You click a crest because you're reading a match and want to know what else this club has produced. Sending you to a full page throws away what you were doing. 12a puts it in the same Inspector the match uses — so the wall stays behind it, and closing returns you exactly where you were. BUILD.md §4 already said every sheet becomes this panel; a club is a sheet on mobile.
LISTS VS THE HUNT
They look alike and are not. A list is something you wrote — arbitrary, personal, shareable. A collection is something the product defined and you're completing. So lists get an author and a share control; collections get a progress ring and a completion reward. 12b and 12c keep that distinction visible.
THE HUNT EARNS A WALL
Mobile shows collections as rows and one collection at a time. Desktop shows a collection's whole grid — collected and missing side by side — which is what makes a gap feel like a gap.
11 The P1 surfaces — rating fast, and the three views

From BUILD.md §1. Four surfaces a user reaches off the main path: quick-rate, the skin and share composer, search results, and the activity feed. The first needed a real decision — mobile's quick-rate hangs off the gold nav diamond, and web has no nav diamond, so the trigger had to be reinvented rather than translated.

QUICK-RATE HAD NO TRIGGER
On the phone the gold diamond sits in the nav, always reachable, and opens a sheet. Web's nav is a horizontal bar — putting a diamond in it would be a phone affordance pasted onto a desktop chrome. Instead the trigger is the wall itself: hover a card, press R, and a compact dialog opens over it. You never leave the grid, which is the thing desktop is good at.
It still resolves into 7e, like every other rating path (BUILD.md §4.9.1).
KEYBOARD, SETTLED HERE
§2.1 was a list of suggestions. 11a fixes the four that matter and shows them in the dialog itself: 1–5 to rate, . for half stars, C to stamp a Classic, ⏎ to log. Shown, not documented — a shortcut nobody discovers is a shortcut that doesn't exist.
SHARE IS A COMPOSER
Mobile picks a skin, then shares. Desktop can show the skin and the output at once — pick on the left, watch the 4:5 and 16:9 render live on the right. One screen instead of two, because there's room for the preview to be real size.
10 People on web — two phone screens become one

Mobile needed 9a for the list and 9b for finding someone, because a phone has one column and the two jobs can't share it. Desktop can hold both at once — and that is the difference worth exploiting, not a wider copy of either. 8b's counters were the same flat text mobile's were; they now point here.

WHY ONE SCREEN
Finding people and managing who you follow are the same activity interrupted by a screen boundary. On a phone you tap out to search, then tap back to check you aren't already following them. Side by side, the list is the check — and the suggestion column can show you someone while their name is still visible in the list you already have.
Both halves keep the mobile rules verbatim: sorted by agreement, no percentage below ten shared matches, and no follower counts anywhere — popularity is not a reason to follow an opinion.
WHAT DESKTOP ADDS
One thing, and only because there is room for it honestly: the agreement bar per row. The phone shows a percentage and a sentence; here the five-step ramp fits inline, so twenty rows become scannable by colour without anyone reading a number.
The number still ships beside it — heat never appears as colour alone.
GOLD, PER PANEL
The list panel and the suggestion panel each get their own budget (BUILD.md §2.1). In the list that is nothing — every row is already followed. In the suggestions it is the Follow buttons, which is exactly what the panel is for.
8 The screens that were blocking a build

From BUILD.md §1. Catalog, Profile and CompetitionBody are real functions in RankItWeb.jsx with no reference — a developer would invent a language or leave them on the old one, which is exactly how a surface ends up half-migrated. 7 The web surface — a translation, not a port

Read from frontend/src/rankit/web/. The web already has its own shell — sticky header, left Rail, an auto-fill wall, and a minimisable Inspector where the phone has bottom sheets. So this is not a port: every sheet becomes a panel, and the three things desktop genuinely does better get real screens instead of wider copies of phone ones.

WHAT CARRIES OVER UNCHANGED
✓The card: same chamfer, same 22px notch hairlines that turn gold on a Classic, same 68° sheen, same crest geometry. Web already shares ri-match-card with the phone — that stays.
✓Heat as the only new palette, always with its number beside it.
✓Gold marks one thing per region. On web the region is a panel, not a viewport — the rail and the inspector each get their own budget.
✓Rajdhani when the product speaks, Outfit when a person does. Spoiler shield, respect, the seven skins: identical rules.
FOUR DECISIONS YOU MADE
WALL AT 320
Up from minmax(268px,1fr). Everywhere, not just the shelf — so a skin is recognisable in every grid it appears in.
OVERLAY FOR 7e
The collectible moment is full-screen over a dimmed wall. The inspector is already a panel; resolving a rating inside it would make the payoff smaller than the act.
COMPANION IN THE INSPECTOR
Third tab, same as the phone. Desktop could split match and room side by side; keeping them in one panel keeps one mental model across both surfaces.
FULLY RESPONSIVE
1080 drops the rail to icons, 820 drops the inspector to a full-width sheet — the phone's own pattern, reached by narrowing.
WHAT DESKTOP EARNS
7f The shelf as a real wall. A hundred cards at once. On a phone the shelf is a promise; here it is the thing itself.
7g Season heat map. Thirty-eight weeks in one view. Physically impossible at 375px.
7h Two-column review reading. Long opinions with the match kept in view beside them.
Everything else is the phone's logic in a wider frame — which is correct, not lazy.
